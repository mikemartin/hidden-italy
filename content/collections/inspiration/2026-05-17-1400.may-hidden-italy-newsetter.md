        text: "\_Wrapped around a fishing port on the north-western tip of Sardinia, Alghero is one of the most attractive towns on the island. When the locals rose up against their Spanish masters in the 14th century, they were driven out and the town was repopulated by settlers from around Barcelona, giving the town its nickname: Barceloneta (Little Barcelona). Six centuries later, Alghero retains strong links to Spain: an archaic form of Catalan is still wildly spoken here, there is a permanent delegation from Catalonia and Barcellona is the most popular soccer team in town."

        text: "\_Although the city is built archaeological remains that date from the Neolithic era, the city itself was founded in the beginning of the 12th century, when the Doria, a powerful merchant family from Genova, built a fortification overlooking the port to their flourishing trade routes. In 1354, the town passed under control of the kingdom of Aragon, is what is now the north-eastern corner of Spain. Although the Spanish lost control of the town in the early 1700s, their influence and language has remained."

        text: "\_Alghero is still a flourishing fishing port, which means it has resisted the excesses of mass-tourism, maintained its relaxed charm and serves some of the finest seafood in Italy. With its historic centre still intact, surrounded by a medieval wall with eight original lookout towers, a spectacular coastline and a fascinating hinterland, Alghero makes a good destination any time of the year, but particularly in autumn, when the crowds have gone home, the sea is still warm and you can enjoy the long barmy evenings."

The Tenuta Sella & Mosca (www.sellaemosca.com), a short eleven km drive out of Alghero, is one of the most innovative and celebrated wine producers on the island. Founded over 100 years ago by Eriminio Sella and Edgardo Mosca, the beautiful central compound is surrounded by over 1200 acres of vineyards, making it one of the largest producers in Europe.
